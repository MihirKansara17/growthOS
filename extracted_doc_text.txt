MASTER PROJECT DOCUMENT

AI Decision Intelligence for Indian Retail Businesses

From business data → diagnosis → prediction → simulation → recommendation → measurement

Project identityGrowthOS is proposed as a domain-specific, AI-assisted decision-intelligence platform for digitally active and growing Indian retail businesses. It is designed to go beyond dashboards: the system should explain why business performance changed, identify risks and opportunities, predict likely outcomes, simulate decisions, recommend evidence-backed actions, and measure the results.

1. Executive Vision

1.1 The one-line idea

GrowthOS is an AI-assisted decision-intelligence platform that turns retail transaction, customer, product and inventory data into evidence-backed business decisions.

1.2 The core loop

DATA → OBSERVE → DIAGNOSE → PREDICT → SIMULATE → RECOMMEND → ACT → MEASURE

The product should not be positioned as a generic AI chatbot or another dashboard. Its purpose is to close the loop between business data and business action.

1.3 Product thesis

Most merchants can see numbers but struggle to interpret the drivers behind them.

Business decisions often involve trade-offs: revenue versus margin, discount versus inventory, acquisition versus retention.

AI is valuable when it reduces the effort needed to investigate and decide, not when it merely generates text.

The source of truth should remain the merchant's structured data and deterministic analytics.

The LLM should operate as an interaction, reasoning and explanation layer over approved tools.

Every important recommendation should be inspectable: evidence, time window, assumptions and confidence.

7. AI Strategy — Use Models, Build the System

The project does not require training a proprietary LLM. The recommended approach is to use a foundation-model API, such as OpenRouter, while building the domain-specific intelligence system around it.

7.1 Recommended AI architecture

React  ↓FastAPI /api/v1/ai/chat  ↓AI Orchestrator  ↓LLM API / OpenRouter  ↓Approved tool call  ↓GrowthOS analytics / ML / database services  ↓Structured evidence  ↓LLM explanation  ↓Validated response  ↓React

7.2 What the LLM should do

Interpret natural-language business questions.

Select approved business tools.

Ask for missing parameters when required.

Summarize retrieved metrics.

Explain trends and evidence in plain language.

Interpret scenario results.

Generate concise business briefs.

Produce recommendation explanations.

7.3 What the LLM should not do

Directly execute arbitrary SQL.

Invent numbers.

Decide authorization.

Retrieve another merchant's data.

Receive raw personal/payment data unnecessarily.

Perform deterministic KPI calculations when the backend can do them.

Execute consequential actions without explicit merchant confirmation.

8.2 Forecast evaluation

Use time-based train/test splits.

Compare against naive and seasonal-naive baselines.

Track MAE, RMSE and an appropriate percentage/weighted error metric.

Show uncertainty intervals.

Store model version and prediction horizon with every forecast.

8.3 Churn risk

Define churn according to the retail purchase cycle. Do not claim that the model knows who will churn. Evaluate precision, recall, PR-AUC and calibration, and account for the cost of false positives and false negatives.

8.4 Anomaly detection

Start with transparent rolling baselines and thresholds. Advanced methods should only be introduced if they reduce false alerts or identify meaningful events that simple methods miss.

25. Technical Defense / Viva Preparation

Question

Answer

Why not just use spreadsheets?

GrowthOS standardizes definitions, automates recurring analysis, protects access, connects multiple intelligence layers and surfaces evidence-backed decisions in one workflow.

Why use an LLM?

For natural-language interaction, tool selection, explanation and summarization—not for deterministic KPI calculations or authorization.

Why OpenRouter?

It provides an API layer for accessing foundation models while allowing the application architecture to remain model-agnostic.

Did you build your own LLM?

No. The project focuses on building the domain-specific decision system around a foundation model.

What is your AI contribution?

Tool orchestration, domain prompts, structured outputs, evidence grounding, authorization, guardrails, evaluation and integration with analytics/ML services.

Where is ML used?

Only where a predictive task provides measurable value, such as forecasting or demand/churn risk.

Why FastAPI?

Python-friendly APIs make it straightforward to integrate analytics, ML and the LLM orchestration layer.

Why PostgreSQL?

Relational business data has strong consistency, joins, constraints and transactional behavior suitable for merchant analytics.

How do you prevent hallucinations?

The model receives approved structured tool results, not unrestricted database access; responses are constrained by evidence and the system should expose limitations.

How do you prevent cross-merchant access?

Backend authorization and merchant-scoped queries on every protected operation, backed by security tests.

What is the hardest technical part?

Turning heterogeneous business data into trustworthy, explainable decision signals and connecting those signals safely to an LLM.

What makes this more than a dashboard?

Root-cause diagnosis, forecasting, scenario simulation, recommendation ranking, evidence and a closed decision-measurement loop.
